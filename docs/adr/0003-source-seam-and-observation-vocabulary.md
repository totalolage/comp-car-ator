# Source seam: `harvest(ctx) → AsyncIterable<Observation>` over a closed Observation union

Every Source adapter satisfies one interface, `Source { id; scope?; harvest(ctx) }`, and emits Observations from one closed union: `ListingSeen | ListingPriceSeen | ListingGone | FxRate | CatalogEntry | VariantFacts | ListPrice | SliceDone`. The context gives the adapter everything it may touch: the Http module, the harvest mode (incremental or full), a read-only Checkpoint for the run's scope (`lastRunAt`, `isKnown(ref)`, `sliceDoneAt(slice)`, `offersInForce()`), the run's clock, a logger and an abort signal. Observations carry no source id and no timestamp; the Ledger stamps both from the run. This is the one external seam with many adapters (Sauto, TipCars, Bazoš, AAA Auto, Autoesa, AutoScout24, mobile.de, the vehicle register, EEA, Wikidata, price lists, ČNB), so its depth decides how much each Wave 1 stream has to know: an adapter author learns one method and eight Observation shapes, and everything else (paging, parsing, query slicing, stop rules) stays inside the adapter.

## Contract

- Lazy: no request before the first `next()`. Duplicates are allowed; the Ledger is idempotent.
- `full` means census of the adapter's `scope`: it yields a ListingSeen for every listing in that scope or throws. Census inference only touches listings last seen under the same scope, so adapter instances with different scopes (e.g. `scope: "de"` and `"at"`, or a narrowed test instance) never retire each other's listings. A thrown harvest infers nothing, and a full run with any rejected Observation ends the census without inferring absence, so a broken parser cannot mark the site gone. Misses count at most once per day, so re-running a full harvest the same day is harmless. The same census covers a Source's CatalogEntries: an entry in the scope that no run of the census stated is retired at once (one miss, since a catalog census reads one deterministic dataset), so a Variant re-keyed between censuses leaves no duplicate; stating it again restores it (ADR 0011).
- A census can span runs. An adapter that splits its scope into slices (AutoScout24 queries by Condition, then bisected whole-euro price band (below), then first-registration year, under the 4,000-result cap; its make filters miss listings, so make only narrows a scope given makes) yields `SliceDone { slice }` after each finished slice, with its own stable slice key. The Ledger records it durably in order, so a later failure keeps it. The full runs of a scope since its last complete full run form the current census: `checkpoint.sliceDoneAt(slice)` returns when one of them finished the slice, and the next full run skips such slices. The complete full run that ends the census counts a listing as missed only when none of the census's runs saw it. A slice with a rejected Observation is not done (its SliceDone is rejected), so it is harvested again. A re-read that checks a slice (a second pass, a recount) must reach the site, so harvests run without a response cache (ADR 0014); a slice that stays short of the site's count is not done. After any complete full run every slice starts null, even when rejections kept it from inferring absence: otherwise a persistently broken slice would keep the census open and the finished slices skipped indefinitely. An adapter may also redo a slice it judges stale. A slice key may also mark a sighting rather than a part of the scope: TipCars yields `SliceDone("listing:<ref>")` for each new or demo listing a slice read whole from its detail page, so a later run of the census that pages that slice again (to count a split slice) needs no detail page for it. Such a mark records a detail sighting within the current census only. It follows its containing slice's checkpoint, right after that slice's SliceDone (a rejection counts towards the next SliceDone, so a mark never takes the slice's), but it is a checkpoint of its own: it clears when the census ends, as every slice's does, and is consulted only when a finished slice is paged again; a slice whose SliceDone was rejected is harvested again with its detail pages. The adapter trusts a mark only while the Ledger knows the listing (`isKnown(ref)`), so one gone since is read again.
- AutoScout24's price bands are disjoint and in whole euros: the site's price filter takes whole-euro bounds, both inclusive, and a fractional bound matches nothing, so a band is halved into [from, mid] and [mid + 1, to]. Known limitation: no band takes a listing without a price, nor one priced between mid and mid + 1 of a band halved there. On 2026-10-27 (fixtures/price-filter.cassette.json) every price sampled was a whole euro, price 0 matched nothing, and every Condition counted as many listings as its two top bands (DE used 633,456 = 633,404 + 52), so none was unpriced. Should such a listing appear, the recount of the slice whose bands miss it comes out short and the census fails with "used (1 listing outside every whole-euro price band, possibly unpriced or fractional, or counts that moved)", or "possibly fractional" for a band halved around its price, rather than count it as harvested. The remedy then is a slice of that Condition without a price filter, or one spanning the gap, narrowed by other filters (year, make) until it fits under the cap, so the missing listings are harvested and the recount counts them. Meanwhile the incremental run before every Census day (ADR 0014) still brings in new listings, so a census stuck this way fails loudly rather than going stale; what waits for it is absence inference and price changes of known listings. The recount's counts are taken one after another, never at one instant. The parts are counted there and back, each keeping its lower count, so one listing moving between bands meanwhile counts in one at most; two moving opposite ways within those few requests, or one moving there and back, can still offset a missed listing in that recount. A missed listing outlasts the moves: the next census's recount finds it again.
- `incremental` may stop early (newest first, stop at `isKnown`). The Ledger never infers absence from incremental runs. An incremental run that meets a known listing in list data carrying its price may yield `ListingPriceSeen` for it, so its price changes land the day they show (daily price history, ADR 0012) without a detail page each; the list price must be the amount the Source's ListingSeen would carry, in the same currency (a basis change waits for the next ListingSeen, see `ListingPriceSeen` below). Otherwise price changes of known listings come from full runs, so each listing Source needs a full-run cadence (weekly or better). Such a price sweep may be bounded: TipCars' walks both lists to their ends each day while they fit its request cap, and rotates over them past it, so every known listing is priced within a stated number of days rather than daily (ADR 0014).
- All network access goes through `ctx.http`, passing `ctx.signal` so aborts interrupt in-flight requests; the clock only through `ctx.now`. That is what makes the adapter testable through `harvest` with the replay Http adapter and a fixed `now`.

## Considered options (design it twice)

- **A1, chosen: one async-iterable `harvest` per adapter, closed union, run-stamped envelope.** One seam, one code path in the Ledger and runner. Streaming lets the Ledger batch transactions and lets the runner abort.
- **A2: two-phase adapter `listPages(query) → raw` + `parse(raw) → Listing[]`** (the shape the scrape research suggested). Parsing becomes testable without Http, but pagination and stopping move into the runner, and every site paginates differently (offsets, per-make categories, query slicing under AutoScout24's 4,000-result cap). The runner would grow per-site branches: shallow adapters, leaky runner. Rejected; adapters can still keep `parse` as an internal function with its own tests.
- **A3: one interface per kind (`ListingSource`, `CatalogSource`, `FxSource`)** with typed returns. The type would say what a Source emits, but it triples the seam and the runner's code paths, and real Sources emit several kinds (Sauto: used and new listings; a manufacturer price list: ListPrice and CatalogEntry). Rejected.
- **A4: push style `harvest(ctx, emit)`.** Simple to write, but no backpressure or natural cancellation, and tests need a fake sink. Rejected.
- **A5: adapter-stamped envelope (`source`, `observedAt` on each Observation).** Every adapter would have to get both right, and the run already knows them. Rejected.

An envoy-peer review (another model family) agreed A1 beats A2–A5 and pushed for the census-scope, rejection and same-day rules above, which are now part of the contract.

### Resumable slices

- **S1, chosen: `SliceDone` in the Observation stream, census spanning the full runs since the last complete one.** Progress travels the same ordered stream as the listings, so a recorded SliceDone implies its listings are recorded, with no second write path. Static `scope` stays the census boundary; slices are progress inside it, so adaptive re-slicing (splitting a slice that grew past the cap) and listings moving between price slices cannot strand or falsely miss listings.
- **S2: per-slice census (listings tagged with their slice, census on each SliceDone).** Resumes too, but a listing whose price moves it to another slice is missed by the old one, and splitting slice A into A1/A2 strands A's absent listings forever. Rejected after envoy-peer review.
- **S3: a write method on the context (`ctx.checkpoint.markDone`).** A second channel into the Ledger, ordered separately from the stream it describes. Rejected.
- **S4: one adapter instance per static slice.** Cannot follow adaptive slicing, which the result cap forces. Rejected for AutoScout24; still fine for coarse splits such as DE/AT.

## Observation vocabulary notes

- `VehicleDescriptor` is the raw description a Source gives (make/model/trim text, source taxonomy ids, registration, specs it states); it is the Resolver's input, together with the SourceId that namespaces the taxonomy ids. Sources map their own labels onto the closed value sets and leave a field undefined rather than guess.
- `CatalogEntry` fully identifies a Variant and is the only kind that creates one. `VariantFacts` carries partial Spec evidence from Sources that cannot identify a Variant on their own (EEA has no body, Wikidata is sparse): the Ledger resolves it at snapshot time and fills Specs the CatalogEntries left empty, never overriding them. Catalog Sources therefore never guess missing identity fields.
- `ListPrice` is a new price of one Offer in one edition. Offer identity is not Variant identity: several Offers (Trims, FWD/AWD, packs, model years) resolve to one Variant, and none of the descriptor fields reliably tells them apart, so the Source gives each Offer a stable `ref` (sales code or curated row key, unique within the Source). The Ledger keys editions by (source, ref, `validFrom`); a later edition replaces earlier ones of its Offer, never another Offer's. `validTo` (inclusive) ends an edition: from the day after, the Offer has no New price, and an older edition never comes back. To withdraw an Offer the Source re-emits its current edition with `validTo`; an Offer simply missing from a list stays current. Manufacturers publish only the current edition of a price list, so the Source learns what the previous one held from `checkpoint.offersInForce()`: per ref, the latest edition, unless it has a `validTo`. A price-list adapter withdraws only in full runs, and only Offers of a model whose lists it fetched and read cleanly in that run; ADR 0013 gives the `validTo` it sets and when a model counts as gone from the brand's index. A list that fails to read therefore withdraws nothing. The alternative, the Ledger inferring withdrawal from full runs as it does for listings, was rejected: the Ledger cannot tell which document an Offer came from, so one unreadable PDF would retire a whole model. Dealer new and demo cars are `ListingSeen` with `condition` new/demo, not ListPrice.
- `ListingSeen.sellerExtras` carries charges the seller invoices on top of the price (delivery, preparation, dealer fees), in the price's currency and with their own price basis, since a net car price can come with gross fees. Pricing normalizes them like the price and puts them in the CZ VAT base of a new car; the asking price stays unchanged.
- `ListingSeen.inspectionValidUntil` carries a shown German/Austrian inspection date into the landed price.
- `ListingPriceSeen` (2026-10-28, PR #69) is a price-only sighting of a listing the Ledger knows: the Ledger updates its price and price history and nothing else. It leaves `last_seen` and the census bookkeeping alone, so a price-only sighting never counts as a census sighting and gone inference is unchanged. A ref with no live listing, or a price in another currency than the listing's, is rejected, since only a full ListingSeen adds a listing, revives a gone one or changes its currency. The list price carries no basis, so a listing that switches between showing its price with and without VAT keeps its old `priceBasis` until its next ListingSeen; the history records the shown amount either way, as it would from a ListingSeen. Considered instead: `Checkpoint.knownPrice(ref)` with a detail page wherever the list price differs, which keeps the union closed and the basis exact but costs a detail page per price change; rejected in favour of a sighting other list-page Sources (TipCars, Bazoš) can also yield.
- `FxRate` is per single unit (`kurz / množství`), so per-100 quotes are normalized at the Source.
- Adding an Observation kind is a breaking change to the seam: extend the union, the Ledger's validation and recording, and this ADR together.
