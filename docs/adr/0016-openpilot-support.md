# openpilot support from comma's own list, placed on Variants precision first

Buyers who want comma.ai's openpilot driver assistance (a comma 3X on the windscreen) need to know whether a car can run it. comma publishes the answer as a generated table, so the catalog shows it from there: a badge and a facet on catalog rows, and a section on the model page. A car wrongly shown as supported costs a buyer more than one not shown, so every step below drops a row rather than guess.

## Source

The `comma-openpilot` Source reads two generated Markdown files over plain HTTPS:

- `commaai/opendbc` `docs/CARS.md`, the current inventory: every known car with its package and support level (upstream, community, custom, under review, dashcam, not compatible). It is generated from opendbc's car definitions and matches comma.ai/vehicles. As of opendbc 8ceee62 (2026-10-05) it lists 406 cars, 341 of them upstream.
- `commaai/openpilot` `docs/CARS.md`, the older, detailed rendering of the upstream cars only: longitudinal control, the speeds below which openpilot does not engage or steer, and the harness. Its rows are joined on the exact make, model cell and package. A row without an exact join has no details; a fuzzy join could pin one car's harness on another.

- `commaai/opendbc` `opendbc/car/<brand>/values.py`, the car definitions CARS.md is generated from. Each platform there (`HYUNDAI_TUCSON`, `HYUNDAI_TUCSON_4TH_GEN`) lists the cars it covers under the names CARS.md shows ("Hyundai Tucson 2021"), so each row gets its platform by exact name. Ford's definitions add a "Hybrid" and a "Plug-in Hybrid" car for `hybrid=True` and `plug_in_hybrid=True`; the adapter does the same. A name two platforms claim gets none. Cars defined outside the brands' platforms (`extra_cars.py`: most community and incompatible rows) have no platform. The adapter reads a fixed list of brand directories; a brand comma adds later gives its rows no platform until it is added.

There is no JSON form and no per-car link. comma.ai/vehicles embeds its data in a hashed script bundle, so each row links to its make's section of that page.

The list is a census, like the register: every run reads the whole list, and a full run's rows replace the previous ones. The Ledger stores the rows in their own table and retires the ones a full census no longer states, as it does catalog entries. It runs weekly, since comma regenerates the list every few weeks.

## Observation

`OpenpilotSupport` is one row: make and model as listed, the fuel only when the model name states it ("RAV4 Hybrid", "Kona Electric"), the model years, comma's platform, level, package, market, and the details. `market` is the row's own qualifier ("Europe only", "China only"), or "US", which the list says is its default. `europe` is true when the qualifier names Europe (or "Non-US"), false when it names only other markets, and absent for a US-referenced row, whose European version may differ.

## Placement (Ledger snapshot)

The Ledger places each row at snapshot time, as it resolves VariantFacts and Offers:

1. A row with `europe: false` is not placed.
2. The Resolver places make and model (fuel included, so "Kona Electric" is read as a Kona). An unknown model is not placed. The Resolver takes a longer name for a shorter one it knows ("Corolla Cross" as a Corolla), so the row is placed only when every word the listed name adds to the catalog's is a trim, fuel or body word (GTI, Hybrid, Electric, Hatchback...). Any other word may name a car the catalog lacks.
3. Platform: a row without one is placed on its model only. A platform is one car to openpilot (one harness, one set of firmware), whatever names and years comma lists it under, so all its rows under one model share a Generation, and the platform's years together decide it: "Tucson 2021" and "Tucson Diesel 2019" are both `HYUNDAI_TUCSON`, the third-generation TL; "Tucson 2022" is `HYUNDAI_TUCSON_4TH_GEN`, the NX4. Year overlap alone put the US Tucson 2021 on the NX4, which the register starts in 2021 (Europe got it in late 2020, the US as model year 2022).
4. Generation: the platform's model years are counted against each of the model's Generations (`from`-`to`, open-ended while in production). The Generation holding the most of them is taken if it holds more than any other, more than half of them, and at least two (or the only year). Model years run ahead of production years, so a year two Generations share ("Golf 2020") decides nothing. A platform mostly outside every Generation the catalog has belongs to one it lacks ("Golf GTI 2015-21" against a catalog with only the Golf VIII). The register also files some cars under a wide type around the real one ("Octavia SE 2000-2019" around "Octavia 5E 2013-2020"); when several hold every year of two or more, the one inside all the others, give or take a year at either end (register years are percentiles) and sharing two years at least, is taken if it is the only one; otherwise none.
5. Changeover: a model year within a year of another Generation's production years sits on a changeover, where the US may still sell the old car or already the new one. A row is placed only when at least one of its own years lies in the Generation clear of every changeover; a row whose years all sit on one stays on its model ("Tucson 2021" alone on its platform would; "Kona 2022-23" against the Kona SX2 from 2023; "Sorento 2019" against the Sorento MQ4 from 2020, while its platform's "Sorento 2018" row is placed). A platform with any year in a rival Generation more than a year from its own is two cars and is not placed ("2017-23" against Generations 2015-2020 and 2021-), however the majority falls. Rivals are all the model's other Generations except types nested with it (the Octavia SE around the Octavia 5E; two still-open Generations are two starts, not nested, and a type built only in the year after the Generation ends follows it). Junk and umbrella register types are rivals too: a long type overlapping the Generation may be its real predecessor, so its years decide nothing and its years away from the Generation make the platform two cars. The register's Fabia 5J runs 2007-2022 over the Fabia II and III, so "Fabia 2022-23" stays on its model; the Golf VII rows are placed on their 2018, clear of the Golf 1H (2000-2016) and the Golf VIII (from 2022). An exemption for long types running alongside was tried and dropped after envoy-peer counterexamples: it let real changeovers through and placed nothing more on the real catalog.
6. Variants of that Generation that were built in at least two of the row's model years (or in its only one, or in their only one), and registered no more than a year before their Generation began (an Audi A3 8P engine filed under the 8V from 2009). A Variant that ends or starts in a row's boundary year is usually the design before or after (the Superb II, filed with the Superb III, ending in 2015). On a Generation running more than eight years up to the platform's last year, longer than one design (the register's Tiguan 5N runs 2008-2024, the Mazda 3 BL 2009-2018 holds the BL and the BM), a Variant must also have started no more than a year before the platform's first model year: one built since 2008 there is the earlier car, and one from 2015 runs across both (a Touran diesel registered 2014-2017 spans the Touran I and II). This drops some Variants of the later car too (Tiguan II engines from 2016 for a list that starts in 2018). Register types these rules cannot split are listed in the matcher and take no row: the Civic FK files the Civic VIII, IX and X hatchbacks under one type, often at one power. The fuel must fit too: a row stating a fuel takes only that fuel. A row stating none takes only the combustion fuels (petrol, diesel, LPG, CNG), unless every Variant of the Generation is electric or hydrogen (Ioniq 5, ID.4, Nexo); a hybrid-only Generation (the European Corolla) is left to the row that names the hybrid. comma lists electrified versions under their own names ("Kona Electric", "Kuga Plug-in Hybrid", Volkswagen's "GTE" and "eHybrid"), and the US "Explorer" row says nothing about the European Explorer EV.

Each Variant then shows the best level among the rows placed on it, with the model years the list gives that level for. The Kona gets "upstream, 2020", not a bare "upstream" over 2022-23 cars the list rates dashcam. The model page shows every placed row with its package, years, market note and link. The facet filters on the Variant's level, with "unlisted" for a Variant no row reached.

## Considered options

- **New Observation vs VariantFacts.** VariantFacts fills missing Specs on one resolved Variant; openpilot support is not a Spec, spans Variants and years, and needs census retirement. Reusing it would change what VariantFacts means. Chosen: a new kind.
- **Placement in the Publisher vs the Ledger snapshot.** The snapshot already resolves VariantFacts and Offers against the merged catalog with the Resolver; doing the same here keeps one place that knows how Observations land in the catalog, and the Publisher only projects. Chosen: the snapshot, in a private module (`packages/ledger/src/openpilot.ts`) tested through `record`/`snapshot`.
- **A curated row-to-Generation table.** Exact, but 341+ rows to maintain by hand as comma adds cars. Rejected in favour of the conservative rule above, checked against the real catalog (matched and unmatched rows and a hand-checked sample, recorded in the PR). A deny list stays the remedy if a wrong placement turns up.
- **Year overlap alone vs comma's platform.** The first version placed each row by its own years. comma's model years are mostly US ones, which can trail Europe's by a year or two at a changeover, so a boundary year landed on the wrong Generation (Tucson 2021 on the NX4) and the Publisher showed its years there. Chosen: the platform, read from the definitions CARS.md is generated from, with changeover years deciding nothing.
- **Every platform year clear of a changeover.** Stricter, but a single noisy register type near a Generation (the "0s" Kona the register files beside the Kona OS) then blocks every row of it: 72 rows placed on the real catalog against 129. Rejected for: one of the row's years clear, the platform's years choosing the Generation.
- **A curated platform-to-Generation table.** Exact, but platform names are comma's and Generations the register's; neither carries the other's ids, and comma adds platforms every release. The platform rule and the deny list stay the remedy.
- **Taking every Generation with three or more of the row's years.** Would cover a facelift the register files as its own Generation, but ADR 0011's Generations follow type approvals and registration years, so a large overlap does not prove the same platform. Rejected after envoy-peer review: one Generation per row.
- **Unstated fuel matches every fuel, or every fuel its sibling rows do not name.** The first puts the US Explorer's rating on the European Explorer EV. The second (the plain "Kona" leaves out the fuels "Kona Electric" names) was the first draft; the envoy-peer pointed out that a sibling establishes its own coverage, not the complement of another row. Chosen: an unnamed fuel is the combustion car.
- **One "compatible" flag.** The list's levels differ in what a buyer can do (plug and play, a community fork, dashcam only), so all six are kept; the badge shows only upstream and community.

## Consequences

- Most rows reference US cars. The badge reads as "comma lists this model and year as supported" and the model page says which market the list refers to. European equipment can differ (radar, camera, steering rack), so the page tells the reader to check on comma.ai.
- The package matters and a Variant does not know it ("Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC) & Lane Assist"): the model page shows it next to the level.
- Bundle format: `VariantRow.openpilot` and `ModelFile.openpilot` are optional fields an older reader ignores, so `BUNDLE_FORMAT` stays 3.
