# mobile.de is an experimental, T2-only Source sliced by price

mobile.de (Germany's largest listing site, ~1.5M cars) sits behind Akamai Bot Manager. From the home residential IP (Vodafone CZ, 2026-10-26) Akamai denied every browser an automation library launched (Patchright driving the system Chrome, with Playwright's default switches, with none at all, without locale emulation; Camoufox 156 with GeoIP and humanized input) and let through the same Chrome started as a plain process and attached over CDP afterwards: cold or warm profile, headed under Xvfb, new tab per URL, about 25 search pages in a row. It is not an IP block and not a Linux/Xvfb rejection. One detail matters: Chrome started with `--remote-debugging-port=0` reports `navigator.webdriver` true, and Akamai denied that too (6 of 6 tries) while the same Chrome on an explicit port passed. So Http's T2 `patchright` engine now starts Chrome itself on a free explicit port and attaches (ADR 0005), and `mobiledeSource()` (`mobile-de`) reads mobile.de through it. The Source is registered and runs from the ingest CLI with `CCA_HTTP_BROWSER=patchright` under `xvfb-run`; no schedule runs it.

## Considered options

- **Official Search API** (`services.mobile.de`): account-based, for dealers and partners; no access for a private app.
- **T0/T1** (fetch, Impit): 403 from Akamai on every request, homepage included.
- **Camoufox**: denied (403) like launched Chrome; it stays an optional engine for other sites.
- **Paid proxies, captcha solvers**: out (the project's $0 line).

## Consequences

- Experimental: Akamai's judgement can change, and reputation decays under load. After ~45 page loads in ~90 minutes, including the denied attempts above, the same client got Akamai's interstitial (`sec-if-cpt-container`), slowed responses and denials for a while, also with the setup that had passed; two hours later that setup passed again. Http treats the interstitial as a challenge, so its circuit breaker stops the run after three blocked requests. Pace matters too: through the adapter, 39 documents about 6 s apart went through and the 40th was denied, while sessions of ~25 documents 10–15 s apart passed. So the adapter asks for 15 s between documents, and a run should be scoped with `makes` (`CCA_MOBILEDE_MAKES=1900,3500 pnpm ingest run --source mobile-de …` from the CLI).
- Scale: German cars 2000+ are ~1.24M listings. A query serves 100 pages of 20 (2,000 results), so a full census is ~62,000 T2 pages, some 11 days of runs at that pace, resumable through SliceDone; incremental runs see ~35,000 new listings a day. Useful scopes are a few makes.
- Slicing is Condition → price bands → (a band of one or two euros over the cap only) make. AutoScout24 first split by make (Condition → make → price) and recounted a make split by summing per-make counts, until its makes turned out to miss listings (it now splits by price, then year, ADR 0003); on mobile.de totals move by tens of listings a minute, so summing ~100 make counts of a 1.2M slice over a quarter of an hour would often miss `max(before, after)` by churn alone and fail the census. Price bands cover a slice by construction instead: every listing has a price (`p=0:` matched the unfiltered total within that churn) and bounds are inclusive whole euros (`p=10000:10000` alone found ~2,400). The site reads `.` in `p` as a thousands separator (`p=0:122.99` returned cars up to 12,299 €), so cents cannot be expressed; neighbouring bands share their bound instead (`0-7812`, `7812-15624`), which leaves no gap for a price with cents and only repeats the listings on a bound. A band of one or two euros is not halved further, since its halves would leave out the cents between them. Each band's paging is still checked against its own total. Only such a narrow band with more than 2,000 listings (10,000 € alone has ~2,400) splits by make, with AutoScout24's recount; the taxonomy includes "Andere" for makes outside it.
- Demo cars have no filter of their own (`con` is NEW or USED): the USED slice's Vorführfahrzeug and Tageszulassung listings are demo, as AutoScout24's offer types are. A price is `gross` only when the card shows the net price beside it ("MwSt. ausweisbar"); otherwise `unknown`.
- Search pages carry their data in the Next.js flight payload, not in an API; a site redesign breaks the parser, which then throws "page changed" rather than emitting nothing.
- Fixtures are live runs of the adapter through Http T2 (2026-10-26), in three sessions 80 and 20 minutes apart: `full.cassette.json` is Aiways with a 4-result cap, 16 documents, whose 8 used cars split into price bands down to 15,624 € wide; `incremental.cassette.json` is a first incremental run for Lexus (22 used cars online that day, two pages, three promoted listings on page 1); `incremental-known.cassette.json` is Lexus again with those listings known but the three newest, so the run pages past the unseen ones and stops at a page of known ones. Tests derive the remaining cases from them (short slice, done slices, denial, changed page). Only the make split is not live: reaching a one- or two-euro band over the cap takes some 35 bisection pages, and its recount then counts every make of the taxonomy (~186) up to three times, far more than Akamai lets through in a session. `model-make-split.cassette.json` was recorded by the adapter against a model of the site serving real listings captured during the research with the site's query rules (packages/sources/src/mobilede/mobilede.test.ts).
